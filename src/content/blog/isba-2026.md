I had the chance to attend ISBA 2026 with the support of my advisor, Prof. Ohn. It was a great experience as a junior researcher.

Among the many interesting talks and poster sessions, I was particularly interested in Prof. Khan’s talk on Sustainable AI.

The first statement that caught my attention was: “The Bayesian Learning Rule is as cheap and accurate as Adam.”

The idea is to view Bayesian inference not just as a tool for uncertainty quantification, but as a general learning principle that can be computationally competitive with standard optimizers while preserving distributional information.

Another interesting idea was Posterior Correction.

In online variational Bayes <a href="#ref1" class="reference-link">[1]</a>, we typically use the current approximate posterior as the prior for the next update. Given a new loss \\(\ell_{t+1}\\), the generalized Bayesian update takes the form

<div class="blog-equation">
$$
q_{t+1}(\theta)
\propto
q_t(\theta)
\exp\left(-\eta \ell_{t+1}(\theta)\right).
$$
</div>

The Donsker–Varadhan variational formula <a href="#ref2" class="reference-link">[2]</a> allows us to view this Bayesian update as an optimization problem:

<div class="blog-equation">
$$
q_{t+1}(\theta) = \arg\min_{q\in\mathcal Q}
\left\{
\mathbb E_{\theta\sim q}
\left[ \ell_{t+1}(\theta)\right]
+
\frac{1}{\eta}
\operatorname{KL}(q, q_t)
\right\}.
$$
</div>

When \\(\mathcal Q\\) is unrestricted, this optimization recovers the exact generalized Bayesian update. When \\(\mathcal Q\\) is a restricted variational family, it instead yields a variational approximation.

However, as new data arrive, the best approximation to the contribution of past data may also change. Therefore, rather than only incorporating new information, we may also want to correct previous approximations <a href="#ref3" class="reference-link">[3]</a>:

<div class="blog-equation">
$$
q_{t+1}
\propto
q_t
\exp(-\hat\ell_{t+1\mid t+1})
\prod_{j=1}^t
\exp\left\{
-\big(
\hat\ell_{j\mid t+1}
-
\hat\ell_{j\mid t}
\big)
\right\}.
$$
</div>

I have recently been studying online Bayesian learning, so the talk gave me plenty to think about and was quite valuable to me.
